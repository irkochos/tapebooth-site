How to make a GIF of your screen

A GIF plays by itself, loops, and drops into a chat, a bug report, a slide or a README without a video player. To record a GIF of your screen in Chrome, record a short clip, trim it to the moment that matters, and export it as a GIF. TapeBooth does all three on your computer, free.

Record a GIF in six steps

  1. Add TapeBooth to Chrome. You can skip the camera and microphone on the welcome page: a GIF has no sound.
  2. Open what you want to show, click the TapeBooth button, and choose This tab or Window or screen. Recording just a tab or a window keeps the GIF tightly framed.
  3. Press Start recording, pick the tab or window in Chrome's dialog, and do the thing you want to show. Click rings mark each click so viewers can follow.
  4. Press Stop. The editor opens with your recording.
  5. Trim the start and end to the part you want: move the playhead and choose Start here and End here, or press I and O. Cut out pauses in the middle with Split and Remove part, and blur anything private.
  6. Choose Export, then GIF. Pick the width, the frame rate and the part to export, check the estimated size, and export. The GIF downloads when it's done.

Choosing the settings

A GIF holds up to 600 frames. The dialog asks for a shorter range when a clip is longer.

  • Width: 480, 640 (default) or 960 px. 640 px is sharp enough for most chats and documents; 960 px for detail, at a much bigger file.
  • Size: the dialog estimates the file size and warns above 25 MB, a common upload limit. Shorter clips, a lower frame rate and a smaller width all shrink it.
  • Defaults: set your usual format, width and frame rate under Export defaults in TapeBooth's Settings.

Tips for a GIF people actually watch

  • Keep it short. Five to fifteen seconds that show one thing loop well and stay small.
  • Move the mouse slowly and pause for a beat after each click.
  • Make the window smaller before you record, so text stays readable at 640 px.
  • GIFs use up to 256 colours per frame, so photos and gradients look grainy. For anything long, colourful or with sound, export an MP4 instead.

GIF or MP4?

Choose a GIF for a short, silent loop that should play anywhere without a click. Choose MP4 for anything with sound, anything longer than a minute, or anything that needs to look sharp: it's far smaller for the same length. Both are free in TapeBooth, with no watermark.
